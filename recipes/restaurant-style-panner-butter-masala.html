Restaurant Style Paneer Butter Masala

Description

Paneer Butter Masala, also known as butter paneer, is one of India's most beloved and iconic vegetarian dishes. Originating from North Indian cuisine, this dish features soft, pillowy cubes of fresh paneer (Indian cottage cheese) simmered in a rich, velvety, mildly sweet, and spiced tomato-butter gravy. Its signature luscious texture and harmonious balance of tanginess and warmth make it a staple at celebrations, dinner parties, and restaurants worldwide.

Ingredients

  • 250 grams of fresh paneer, cut into neat, bite-sized cubes.
  • 4 large, ripe red tomatoes, roughly chopped.
  • 2 medium onions, finely sliced.
  • 10 to 12 raw cashew nuts, soaked in warm water for 15 minutes to soften.
  • 1 tablespoon ginger-garlic paste.
  • 3 tablespoons unsalted butter (plus an extra tablespoon for finishing).
  • 2 tablespoons heavy whipping cream or fresh heavy cream.
  • 1 tablespoon oil (to prevent the butter from burning).
  • 1 teaspoon Kashmiri red chili powder (for that vibrant restaurant-style color without excessive heat).
  • 1 teaspoon garam masala powder.
  • 1 tablespoon dried fenugreek leaves (kasuri methi).
  • 1 teaspoon sugar or honey (to balance the natural acidity of the tomatoes).
  • Salt to taste and 1 cup of filtered water.

Steps

  1. First, heat the oil and 1 tablespoon of butter in a large pan over medium heat. Add the sliced onions and sauté them until they turn translucent and soft, then stir in the ginger-garlic paste and cook for another minute until the raw aroma disappears.

  2. Next, add the chopped tomatoes and soaked cashews into the pan, cooking everything together for about 8 to 10 minutes until the tomatoes turn completely mushy and oil begins to separate from the edges. Remove the mixture from the heat, let it cool down slightly, and blend it in a high-speed mixer until it forms a completely smooth, silky puree.

  3. Strain the blended puree back into the same pan using a fine-mesh sieve to filter out any remaining tomato skins or cashew chunks, ensuring a velvety texture for your gravy. Turn the heat to low, stir in the Kashmiri red chili powder, sugar, and salt, and let the sauce simmer gently for 5 minutes.

  4. Finally, gently drop in the fresh paneer cubes along with the remaining butter, heavy cream, and crushed kasuri methi rubbed between your palms. Cover the pan with a lid and let it simmer on low heat for just 3 to 4 minutes so the paneer absorbs the rich flavors without becoming rubbery, then serve hot.
